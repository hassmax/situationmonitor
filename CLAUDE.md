# Global Situation Monitor: notes for Claude Code

A self-updating 3D globe of armed-conflict events, built from public OSINT posts and news feeds, running entirely on free services. The owner is a law student focused on national security, not a developer: explain changes in plain language in PR descriptions, and keep the tool simple, fast, and honest about uncertainty.

## How it runs

- `.github/workflows/update.yml` runs every 15 minutes, at :04, :19, :34 and :49 (GitHub delays and drops scheduled runs at the top of the hour) (plus on pushes to `main` that touch `site/`, `pipeline/`, or the workflow, and manually with an optional `lookback_days` backfill input).
- It restores the whole orphan `data` branch (`state.json`, `events.json`, `archive/`), stopping if the branch exists but cannot be fetched (saving without it would wipe the archive), runs `pipeline/run.py`, force-pushes everything back to `data` as one commit, copies `site/` plus the generated `out/events.json` into `_site/`, stamps `__BUILD__` in `index.html` with the commit SHA (cache-busting), and deploys to GitHub Pages.
- Secrets: `GEMINI_API_KEY` (free Gemini API, OpenAI-compatible endpoint), `TG_API_ID`, `TG_API_HASH`, `TG_SESSION` (Telegram via Telethon). Optional variable `NOMINATIM_EMAIL`. Optional Telegram alerts: secrets `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN`, `TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID`, variable `DASHBOARD_URL`. Never print, log, or commit secret values.
- Free-tier limits matter: Gemini Flash-Lite allows roughly 500 requests/day (the pipeline budgets 470, `daily_llm_calls`, batching up to 40 posts per request, `batch_max_items`, since 2026-10-02 (was 25): the ~4,700-token instructions go with every call; raised from 400 on 2026-09-30); news headlines the model judged irrelevant are remembered for a day and not sent again (`extract.skip_rejected`, `state["rejected_heads"]`); Actions minutes are free because the repo is public. All model use shares one daily counter (`extract.calls_remaining`); calls answered with HTTP 5xx (overloaded or down) or that never reach the provider are not counted (`extract.Busy`), so an outage can't drain the budget; when the chosen model is busy, a run switches once to another model, from `llm_models` (Flash-Lite) and then `llm_fallback_models` (regular Flash, also free, separate smaller limit), and a backup is dropped for the preferred models after `FALLBACK_RECHECK` (1 h): extraction keeps a reserve of `extraction_reserve` (30) calls, the brief is skipped below `brief_min_calls` (5), and the same-story check below `dedupe_min_calls` (10). Every call is also tallied by purpose (`state["llm_calls"]["by"]`: extract, dedupe, recency, brief, split, fleet, probe; `ask_json(..., purpose=)`), and the checks that run after extraction have paced daily shares (`extract.SHARES`, `share_left`: `dedupe_daily_max` 140 (was 90 until 2026-10-02, when it ran out daily with 100 groups waiting and only 281 of 470 calls used), `recency_daily_max` 48, spread over the UTC day plus `SHARE_BURST` 4): on 2026-09-30 the same-story check took most of the then 400 and extraction stopped with 358 posts waiting. New features must not add model calls without budgeting them here (a purpose, and a share if they can run every 15 minutes).

## Layout

- `pipeline/run.py`: orchestrator. Fetch → keyword prefilter → model extraction (batched, budgeted, queued) → geocode (OSM Nominatim, cached) → merge → confidence scoring → write `events.json`.
- `pipeline/sources/`: `bluesky.py` (public API, no login), `telegram.py`, `rss.py` (incl. Google News searches), `gdelt.py` (used only to corroborate, not drawn). The owner's list of leading national outlets (NYT, Xinhua, Der Spiegel, NHK, BBC, Indian Express, Le Monde, TASS, Corriere della Sera, CBC, Grupo Globo, El País, Yonhap, Reforma, ABC Australia, Anadolu, Kompas, NRC, Al Arabiya, NZZ) is read through Google News `site:` searches (`gnews-majors-*`, `gnews-state-wires`, and one per language: `gnews-de/it/nl/es/pt/id`, each in that language's Google News edition with its own keywords, which the prefilter also knows: `_EUROPE_ASIA`, prefilter version 4); all are listed outlets. North Korea and the Korean Peninsula: `gnews-korea` (the Indo-Pacific search needs PLA, coast guard or US Navy in the story). Yemen both ways: Telegram `Alomhoar` (Al-Mehwar News, partisan, side YE, keyword-filtered, Arabic attack words added in prefilter version 5) and `gnews-yemen-coalition` (Arabic search of SPA, Al Arabiya, Al Hadath, Asharq Al-Awsat, all side SA).
- `pipeline/extract.py`: the model prompt (`SYSTEM_PROMPT`), event types, keyword prefilter (`CONFLICT_RE`), model probing and batching. Diplomacy also covers formal changes in international commitments and relations anywhere (leaving, suspending or joining a treaty, alliance or international body; expulsions, recalled ambassadors, cut relations; new sanctions packages against a state, not designations of individuals unless tied to a conflict), in the `global` theater ("Worldwide", no countries, set only by the model, `listed: false`: no row in the theater list, always shown, filtered only by "Diplomacy, legal") when no conflict theater fits, placed at the acting state's capital; searched by `gnews-commitments`; parties may be a body's short name up to 8 letters (GRECO). The type is what happened, not the vehicle: an attack aboard a civilian airliner is hybrid (state blamed or suspected) or not relevant, never airstrike or naval; consequences of an incident (flights suspended, repatriation flights, investigations, "updates") are not events. When words are added to the prefilter, add them as a new `_ADDED_WORDS` version: seen posts only the new words let through get one more look (`state["prefilter_version"]`).
- `pipeline/geo.py`: geocoding, theater assignment, named-sea fallback coordinates. When the model's coordinates and the lookup disagree by 300+ km, the model's stay (approximate) unless a reverse lookup (`country_at`) shows them outside the country it named; then the lookup wins. `repair` re-checks stored approximate pins, `REPAIR_PER_RUN` per run, once per `REPAIR_VERSION`. Region names ("Middle East", "Europe", "Sahel"; `REGIONS`) are never looked up (Nominatim put "Middle East" in Baltimore): they go to a labeled anchor, approximate, for new and stored events (`pin_commands`). For strikes, fighting and naval incidents (`IN_THEATER_TYPES`), a lookup more than `THEATER_KM` (5,000 km) from the theater's camera point is not used (the model's estimate, if in range, is); a theater's `reach_km` overrides it (Indo-Pacific 6,500: Karachi and the Arabian Sea are 5,200-5,500 km from its camera). The model's own coordinates get the same check even when the lookup finds nothing (a sea searched inside the named country finds nothing: "Caribbean Sea" came back as 15, 75, southern India). A place that is just a sea or strait's name (`sea_exact`: "Sea of Japan", "the Red Sea") is never looked up (a lookup limited to the named country found a Sea of Japan in Kawasaki and the Taiwan Strait in Kaohsiung); it goes to the `SEAS` anchor, approximate, for new events, stored events and attack-wave targets (`pin_commands`). An event whose place names a sea in `SEAS` is pinned within `SEA_KM` (2,000) of it, else at its anchor (`_off_sea`; stored events too, in `pin_commands`). The "far outside" log line is written once per place and run.
- `pipeline/recency.py`: drops old stories that arrive with a fresh date. News-only events, attack waves included (not alert groups), are searched for current coverage and coverage 2+ weeks older; with no older on-topic coverage they stay; otherwise current headlines that repeat an older one are set aside as republished copies and the model compares, dropping only when it names the matching older headline. New claims about an old incident count as old; another incident of the same kind does not. The coverage found is recorded (`coverage`); a notable (severity 2+), not corroborated event resting on one source, with 2+ older headlines on its topic (`HOLD_MIN_OLDER`; one is thin evidence for recurring stories) and either no current coverage from another outlet or an article whose own date the date check gave up on (`UNDATED_TRIES`), is flagged `possibly_old` in the published copy (`held`): still on the map, but faded, not animated, never "new", with a "Possibly an old story" note, and no Telegram alert, until a second independent source joins (not hidden: the search can miss current coverage worded differently or of thinly covered places): recurring headlines ("Houthis launched ballistic missiles at Israel") relisted months later get past the model, since another attack of the same kind counts as new. At most one model call per run and at most every 30 minutes unless 8+ events wait. Bump `CHECK_VERSION` when changing the rule (drops from versions below `KEEP_DROPS_FROM` are put back and everything is re-checked).
- `pipeline/datecheck.py`: article date check, no model calls. For notable (severity 2+) events resting on one news report from the last 3 days, and for items from military or government sites whatever their severity (`OFFICIAL`: they post photos and releases long after the fact), up to `PER_RUN` (20) a run, one at a time, none started after `BUDGET_SECONDS` (45; requests time out after `TIMEOUT`, 10 s), most serious and longest waiting first, tried at most `TRIES` (2) times: the Google News redirect is resolved to the publisher (decoded from older ids, else Google's batchexecute endpoint), and the article's own publication date read (published_time meta tags, then JSON-LD datePublished; for photos the earlier "Date Taken" / dateCreated wins). Held carrier positions from articles taken down by a correction are forgotten (`fleet.drop_held`). More than `OLD_DAYS` (14) before the feed's date: dropped (into `state["dropped_as_old"]`, reason logged); earlier than the event by more than `SLACK` (6 h): the event moves to the article's date; unreadable: no change. Each run logs how many articles it could date.
- `pipeline/brief.py`: the "What changed in the last 6 hours" brief (published as `brief` in events.json). One `extract.ask_json` call at most hourly, only when the last 6 hours of events changed, from the dashboard's own corroborated events only (`BRIEF_VERSION` 2; nothing corroborated means no call and the "No new corroborated events" line); event ids the model writes into the text are stripped (`_clean`); bullets citing unknown event ids, or citing uncorroborated events without attribution wording, are dropped; on failure the previous brief stays with its timestamp. Bullets only: per-theater lines were removed because they folded single-source events into statements of fact (`theaters` is always `[]`).
- `pipeline/alerts.py`, `pipeline/config/alerts.yaml`: Telegram alerts (Bot API sendMessage, plain text). Dedupe by (event id, rule) in `state["alerts"]`; first run records everything as seen and sends only "Alerts are on"; at most `max_messages_per_run` messages, else one digest; the bot token must never be logged.
- `pipeline/archive.py`: permanent history on the data branch, `archive/<day>.json` (published events by UTC day of `time`) and `archive/fleet/<day>.json`. Day files are merged, not rebuilt (events aged out of the working set stay; taken-down ids are removed) and written only when their contents change. Keep archive files forever.
- `pipeline/hunter.py`: corroboration hunter. Up to 8 severity-2/3 single-source or one-sided events from the last 12 h get a Google News search (place + type keywords, last day), at most twice per event and 3 h apart (`state["hunter"]`). Results enter the extraction queue at weight 4, credited per outlet like other Google News results (listed outlets count on their own; unlisted ones share the `google-news` group). It must make no model calls.
- `pipeline/corrections.py`, `pipeline/config/corrections.yaml`: hand corrections by event id, each with a required `note`: `hide`, `edit` (summary, place, lat, lon, type, severity), `drop_report` (by URL). Hides and edits are applied to the published copy (reversible, labeled "Corrected"); dropped reports are removed before scoring; reports of hidden events and dropped URLs are filtered out of candidates so nothing is re-created. Event ids are shown at the bottom of the detail view.
- `pipeline/merge.py`: merging reports into events, attack waves, drone and missile alert groups, confidence rules. Diplomacy and legal steps (`TALKS`) match by parties (`_same_talks`: two in common; the same parties with some shared wording; or one list within the other, [PK] and [PK, SA, YE], with closer wording, `TALKS_SUBSET_OVERLAP`) across theaters and across the two types; interchangeable spellings count as one word (`_SPELLINGS`: Makkah, Mecca), and beyond the 400 km radius when the parties are the same and the wording close (`_same_statement`). Deployment reports with the same acting country, the same country concerned, and similar wording (`_same_buildup`) merge however far apart their pins are. In `LOOSE_FAMILIES` (deployment, hybrid, naval, incursion), a report pinned to a whole country, region, or sea (`_broad`) joins a same-country (any country for a sea) report with similar wording (`_same_story`, place words ignored), and two broad pins need similar wording even when close. Attack waves group by attacker and target country, or for launches into the sea the same named sea whatever country the reports give (`_same_target`, `geo.sea_exact`: "Sea of Japan" with JP and "East Sea" with KR are one North Korean launch), within `WINDOW` of the wave's start (no fixed day boundary); unattributed strikes on a wave's places join it. A launch report pinned to the attacker's own territory and naming it after "fired/launched from" ("North Korea fired a ballistic missile from Wonsan", not denials; `merge.launch_sites`, `_launch_report`) is folded into the attacker's wave within `WINDOW` as a named launch area (`origins`), so the line is drawn from there; the prompt also asks for the launch site in origins and the place where the missiles went. A new event's id is the hash of its first report's link; a second event from the same article (a roundup) gets the link plus its summary (`_new_id`), and stored events sharing an id are given their own at load (`unique_ids`; the one with most reports keeps it). `consolidate` folds stored events the rules now join (the earliest id stays; folded ids are removed from the archive); stored strikes and ground fighting fold only with the same place and closely matching wording (`STRIKE_FOLD_OVERLAP`), since broad pins like "Gaza" hold many separate strikes; a strike or fighting report pinned to a whole country joins a same-country event with that close wording and the same names (`_same_broad_hit`, `_names`). Every wording match needs at least `MIN_SHARED` (2) words in common: one shared word once pinned a carrier mishap to Istanbul. For naval events, a report citing UKMTO or JMIC (`MARITIME_AUTHORITY`) leads the headline. Stored diplomacy and legal steps fold with the same parties and closely matching wording (`TALKS_FOLD_OVERLAP`); looser pairs are left to the same-story check.
- `pipeline/dedupe.py`: same-story check. Recent events (72 h, any theater) of one kind in one country are shown to the model as a group, and it lists which describe the same specific incident (follow-up coverage over days included); those are folded into the earliest. Hybrid, deployment, incursion and naval events form one group per country whatever their wording; strikes and fighting only link when wording overlaps; diplomacy and legal steps are grouped by parties (same, one within the other, or two shared, plus some shared wording), not by country or theater, with hybrid and deployment events that name parties (`STATEMENT_KINDS`), and another government's reaction stays separate; an explosion whose summary speaks of sabotage, pipelines, cables and the like (`SABOTAGE_RE`) is also put in its country's hybrid group; a strike or fighting report that speaks of a plan or preparation (`PLAN_RE`) is also put in its country's deployment group; events with no country (pinned to a sea) are grouped by their place name; a name that `STORY_MIN` (4) recent non-talks events share and earlier events (working set and archive) used at most `STORY_BEFORE` (1) times, not a place name, is a new story (`story_names`: "flydubai", one cockpit attack filed as ~15 events of every kind in four countries) and its events form one group across kinds and countries; groups over `MAX_GROUP` are shown as overlapping runs (`_chunks`); groups holding a pair never asked about go before second looks (`NEW`/`SECOND` prefixes on the waiting-since sort key); calls stop when the paced daily share is used (`share`, from `extract.share_left`); waves, alert groups and arms transfers are left out. One `extract.ask_json` call (up to 60 events) every run while groups wait, else at most every 30 minutes (15 after a failure), skipped below `dedupe_min_calls` (10); while groups wait, up to `MAX_CALLS_PER_RUN` (3) calls a run, the extra ones only with `EXTRA_CALLS_FLOOR` (100) calls left. For each folded set the model also writes a combined headline (`headline`, kept while no new report joins; dropped if it has a number the events don't give). Late follow-ups (`late_cases`): events from the last `NEW_HOURS` are also compared with older events up to `LATE_DAYS` back (working set, and the archive via `archive.recent`): hybrid/deployment/incursion/naval with the closest wording, strikes/fighting/talks only with close wording and the same names; a match in the working set is folded (the first date stays), a match only in the archive gives the new event the archived date. Pair answers are remembered 7 days (`state["dedupe"]`, with an answer count `n`); a "different" answer gets one second look after `SECOND_LOOK` (6 h), since a busy preferred model means a weaker backup answers; a group is re-asked only when it has an unsettled pair (`_settled`); the group whose question has waited longest goes first (`_waiting_since`), so big, growing groups can't starve small ones out of the 72 h window.
- `pipeline/fleet.py`: US aircraft carriers (USNI's daily Fleet Tracker feed + movement reports; home-port baseline in `CARRIERS` and `HOME`). News reports (not the tracker) are checked in `_check`: vague places (`VAGUE`) are ignored, a report at another carrier's home port is ignored, a move faster than `MAX_KM_PER_DAY` is held until a second report from a different URL confirms it. `repair` re-checks stored positions once per `FLEET_VERSION`. The tracker text is taken from USNI's feed (article pages return 403 to Actions) and read without the model by `parse_tracker` (hull numbers + named sea areas and ports in `TRACKER_PLACES`; everything said about a carrier is collected first (every sentence naming it, anywhere, plus follow-on sentences in its section about "the strike group"/"the carrier"); then the section title such as "In the Philippine Sea" wins, else any sentence giving a current position, else the departure point; photo captions are skipped; sentences are split into clauses so a departure point ("departed … from Apra Harbor, Guam") or home port ("homeported at Norfolk") is not read as the current position, and a departure point is used, as status "departed", only when nothing gives a current one; the model is only a fallback; each placement is logged with its evidence; bump `TRACKER_PARSER` to re-read the current edition); editions older than `TRACKER_MAX_AGE` don't drive the home-port baseline. The tracker replaces a later news position the carrier couldn't have sailed to from the tracker's (at `MAX_KM_PER_DAY`, 900 km), without drawing a line from it. On the map, the heading line flows toward the destination; the line from the previous position is faint and still. Both follow sea lanes (`SEA`, `SEA_LANES`, `seaPath` in app.js): a line that stays at sea on a coarse land map (`isLand`, `atSea`, built once from the country shapes) is drawn straight; otherwise each end joins the waypoint network wherever it can reach it at sea, and the shortest way through straits and canals (no Panama: carriers don't fit) is drawn. It's the likely route, not a track.
- `pipeline/config/theaters.yaml`, `pipeline/config/sources.yaml`: theaters and sources. If a theater is added or renamed, also update the theater list in `SYSTEM_PROMPT`.
- `site/`: static front end. `index.html`, `styles.css`, `app.js` (vanilla JS, no build step), `vendor/globe.gl.min.js` (2.46.2, vendored), `assets/countries-110m.json`, `data/demo-events.json` (clearly labeled sample data for `?demo`).

## Testing before you push

- Python: `pip install -r pipeline/requirements.txt`, then `python -m pyflakes pipeline` and `python pipeline/run.py --state /tmp/state --out /tmp/out --no-llm` (needs network; skips the model).
- Front end: `python -m http.server --directory site 8000`, open `http://localhost:8000/?demo`. Check the browser console for errors, desktop and a ~390 px mobile width. To test real-shaped data, copy a generated `events.json` to `site/data/events.json`, but never commit that file.
- If startup breaks, the page shows the error in the feed panel (see `fatal()` in `app.js`); keep that behavior.

## Rules that must not be broken

- **Honesty over drama.** Summaries are written by the model in its own words; only summaries and links are published, never full post text. Never add or imply attribution a source doesn't make (a UKMTO "unknown projectile" stays unknown until someone attributes it). The legal layer records stated legal bases; it never assesses them.
- **Confidence rules** (`merge.apply_status`): corroborated = 2+ independent source groups with at least one unaligned (or opposing sides agree); unconfirmed = one unaligned source; claimed = only sources aligned with one side. Sources with the same `side` never corroborate each other. GDELT (3+ outlets nearby) counts as one unaligned source. Google News results count per outlet for outlets listed under `outlets:` in `sources.yaml` (tier 1 = reputable, weight 3; state or partisan media carry a `side`; outlets with one owner or newsroom share a `group`; `names` are other names Google News shows, matched when the link doesn't give the domain (`rss.outlet_named`), and stored reports under a newly listed outlet's name are credited to it every run (`rss.relabel_by_name`); aggregators that republish others, like Yahoo, stay unlisted); unlisted outlets share the `google-news` group, which only counts when no listed outlet has reported the event (syndicated copy must not corroborate its original). Google News reports stored before outlets were told apart carry only the search's name; `rss.relabel` credits them to their outlet by article link (one 7-day re-read of the searches, then from each run's results).
- **Time.** Events are timed by when they happened (`time`, from the model's `happened` field, else the post time). `updated` is only the latest report. The merge window is measured from `time` and must never slide forward with later reports (that bug made old events reappear as new). The front end uses `time` for the window, sorting, and "new". No time may be later than the moment the post was read (`common.make_item` clamps it; `run.py` repairs stored events), because some feeds give date-only stamps set to a UTC midnight that hasn't happened yet.
- **Positions are never estimated.** Carriers show their last reported position with its date; supply routes only use named endpoints (country-level routes, and routes to a US command's region, are drawn faint); an event placed at a US combatant command (CENTCOM and the others) goes to that command's region, labeled and approximate, never its headquarters (`geo.COMMANDS`, `geo.pin_commands`), and a deployment that says it comes "from" a command becomes a movement (`arms_transfer`, supplier = recipient) out of that region to its place, while a country's own ships or aircraft on a port call, visit or exercise abroad are a deployment at the destination, not a route (`merge.own_force_visits`, `VISIT_RE`; Russian warships "arrived in Indonesia for a business visit" were drawn from Vladivostok), and a country's purchases, contracts, approvals or production with its own industry (own forces, not both endpoints named) are arms production, type `production` (`merge.own_procurement`, `PROCURE_RE`), while a purchase or sales deal between two countries with nothing delivered is diplomacy (prompt only); supply routes (and "active") follow the time window, not a fixed 30 days; launch lines from assumed launch areas (`ANCHORS` in app.js, by attacker: RU, UA, IR, YE, LB, IL, KP) are drawn faint, only for drone and missile events (a wave corrected to another type gets none).
- **Map language.** Icon and color show what happened; marker style shows confidence (solid / outline / dashed). Zoomed out, 3+ nearby events collapse into the lead event's icon with a count; closer in, overlapping markers fan out. Only recent events animate (at most `MAX_ANIMATED` at once). Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Warnings that drones or missiles are in flight ("heading toward X", nothing hit) are grouped into one siren marker per country per day; they never become places hit in an attack wave.
- **Performance.** Keep it light: no frameworks, no build step, no new heavy layers. Cap marker and line counts as the code already does. Phones (`PHONE` in app.js) get lighter limits: 160 markers, 8 animations, render resolution 1.5. Land is painted onto the globe's own surface (`paintLand`: an ocean-and-countries canvas used as the globe image, repainted only when highlighted countries change), not drawn as a layer just above it: phone GPUs showed the ocean through such a layer in streaks (no dot or tile pattern either). During a drag or pinch (`moving`), marker layout is throttled, dot and ring sizes are rebuilt only when the gesture ends, and markers don't slide; count bubbles use hysteresis (`CLUSTER_ON` 0.55, `CLUSTER_OFF` 0.45; the selected event is never folded into a bubble). Zoom stops at `minDistance` 106 (globe radius 100), near city scale. Marker size is severity plus scale (`markerPx`: killed, injured, launched; +4 px per tenfold, capped). Over `MAX_MARKERS`, `markerPick` keeps the highest scores (2 × severity + confidence, +2 in the last 6 hours, −1 for diplomacy), not just the newest, and the feed notes how many are left off (`mapLeftOut`); the feed itself shows `FEED_PAGE` (250) at a time with "Show more".
- **Live page.** Every minute, and when the page comes back into view, a HEAD request compares events.json's ETag/Last-Modified with the loaded copy; the file is fetched only when it changed (every `REFRESH_MS` if the headers are missing). New events announce themselves in a short note (`announce`).
- **Search.** Every word must match somewhere in the event (summary, place, type label, countries, sources, region words from `regionWords`: Europe by country, the other regions by theater); a trailing "s" is dropped so plurals match.
- **Filters.** "On the map" (`LEGEND` in app.js) is both the legend and the type filter (`S.off`, keyed by `legendKey`); there is no separate layers panel; "Show everything" and "Hide everything" (`legendChanged`) sit under it, so hiding all and tapping one entry shows only that kind. "Drone or missile" covers attacks, interceptions (air defense, same icon) and the siren warnings. An arms transfer with no route (no supplier or recipient named) is a marker at its own place (`onMap`), not an invisible route. "Arms production" (type `production`, factory icon, teal) is a country's own production and orders from its own industry. Money (grants, loans, fund allocations, compensation; `transfer.money` from the model, else `MONEY_RE` on the wording) is "Financial aid", green, with its own routes, apart from teal arms transfers. Opened events are remembered in `localStorage` (`gsm_viewed`, a week) and stop animating and lose the "new" mark.
- The header reads "Global Situation Monitor" with the byline "By Huzaifa Khan" under it (`.byline`).
- UI copy: plain, sentence case, no jargon. Carrier names start with "U.S.S." (`CARRIERS` in fleet.py). Update `README.md` when behavior changes.

## Owner preferences

- Default time window: 24 hours, opening on the busiest area of activity.
- Everything on one screen (no separate views).
- Coverage priorities: Russia–Ukraine, NATO flank and Russian hybrid attacks, Middle East (incl. shipping in Hormuz and the Red Sea), Sudan and the Horn, eastern DRC and the Sahel, Indo-Pacific, Latin America; diplomacy, legal steps, arms transfers, US carrier movements; worldwide treaty and membership changes, expulsions and sanctions packages.
